\section{Introduction}
Many networks are organized at more than one scale: vertices form local
groups, related groups form larger modules, and the desired output is therefore
a tree rather than one flat partition. Structural entropy supplies an
information-theoretic objective for such an encoding tree \cite{li2016}.
Recent HCSE and BBM methods construct low-entropy hierarchies through recursive
partitioning and balanced merging \cite{pan2025}. Their construction rules,
however, do not answer a complementary question: \emph{after a tree has been
constructed, does a small topology change still lower the same objective?}

The distinction between construction and local optimality matters
algorithmically. A strong constructor can retain avoidable entropy because its
earlier decisions constrain later steps. At the other extreme, unrestricted
tree editing can reconsider many source--destination pairs and require a full
objective recomputation for every candidate. A useful hierarchy optimizer
should therefore combine a broad construction neighborhood with a local
operator whose effect is exact and whose termination is interpretable.

We first introduce Entropy-Guided Tree Editing (\EGTE), a general optimizer for
structural-entropy trees. \EGTE starts from SE agglomeration, recursive SE, and
optionally Paris, then applies exact-guarded level-collapse and subtree-
relocation moves. This general edit space provides a strong hierarchy without
ground-truth labels, but its repeated whole-tree rescoring is expensive. \EGTE
therefore establishes the broad-search component of our framework and exposes
the need for a cheaper certifiable neighborhood.

Nearest-neighbor interchange (NNI) is the elementary rotation of a rooted
binary tree. It changes one internal edge while preserving every leaf. NNI is
standard in phylogenetic tree search and has been studied for other
hierarchical-clustering objectives, but its structural-entropy change has not
been used as an exact graph-hierarchy optimizer. We show that, for
$((A,B),C)\rightarrow(A,(B,C))$, all objective terms cancel except two
cross-module edge weights and three module volumes. This identity yields
monotone descent and a one-NNI local-optimality certificate.

Our full fast method, NNI-certified Entropy Search over Trees (\NEST), refines
several inexpensive initial trees with the exact operator and returns the
lowest independently verified endpoint. A bounded two-move beam can cross a
small intermediate barrier, but accepts a pair only when its endpoint lowers
the full objective. This design retains exact acceptance while avoiding the
costliest part of general tree editing.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \EGTE, a label-free multi-start hierarchy optimizer with
  exact-guarded collapse and subtree-relocation moves;
  \item an exact weighted-graph NNI delta for tree structural entropy, together
  with monotone descent and a checkable one-NNI local certificate;
  \item \NEST, which combines multi-start construction, exact NNI refinement,
  and bounded two-move escape without weakening the output guarantee; and
  \item a paired evaluation against SE agglomeration, Louvain-2L, Paris, and
  the official HCSE and BBM implementations, with entropy, hierarchy recovery,
  runtime, memory, operator audits, and an explicit \EGTE--\NEST ablation.
\end{itemize}

Across five frozen hierarchical graph regimes, \NEST lowers entropy by
$\NESTGainVsHCSE\!\pm\!\NESTGainVsHCSECI$ bits relative to HCSE and by
$\NESTGainVsBBM\!\pm\!\NESTGainVsBBMCI$ bits relative to BBM, winning all
paired comparisons against both. The paper therefore contributes both a
general tree-edit optimizer and a faster locally certified alternative, rather
than treating an implementation artifact as a competing method.
