\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Questions and protocol}
The experiments test the consequences of the exact local identity and the
search procedure derived from it. We ask: (Q1) how often do established
constructors leave an improving NNI;
(Q2) does compound search add improvements beyond a one-step certificate;
(Q3) does lower entropy preserve or improve recovery of a planted hierarchy;
and (Q4) how close is \NEST to the global optimum on exactly solvable graphs?

The sealed main protocol uses 100 graph seeds per regime. Every method sees the
identical weighted graph, and every tree is rescored by the same independent
implementation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:tree-se}. We report paired means and 95\%
$t$-intervals, raw per-seed records, wall-clock time, and Python allocation
peak. The compound search uses beam width 16, barrier $\beta=0.05$ bits, and at
most eight accepted compound rounds. No label is used by \NEST for model
selection. BBM is additionally shown with the planted number of fine clusters,
which is an oracle advantage and is marked as such.

\paragraph{Data.}
Our controlled suite contains five two-level hierarchical stochastic block
models: clean, noisy, imbalanced, weighted, and weak-hierarchy. Each balanced
graph has 64 vertices, eight fine blocks, and four coarse blocks; the
imbalanced regime has the same total size with block sizes from 4 to 12. The
generator manifest, any $0.01$-weight connectivity edges, and every seed are
stored with the results. This family follows the hierarchical stochastic block
model used to study recovery beyond worst-case inputs \cite{cohenaddad2017hsbm}.

\paragraph{Methods.}
We compare SE agglomeration, a two-level Louvain tree \cite{blondel2008}, Paris
\cite{bonald2018paris}, official HCSE, official BBM with oracle $k$
\cite{pan2025}, and \NEST. In the 500-graph benchmark, the \NEST row uses a
fixed pool of three initial trees: SE agglomeration, recursive SE, and Paris.
Each tree is refined independently by exact one-step NNI and bounded two-move
search, and the lowest independently rescored endpoint is returned. HCSE, BBM,
and Louvain-2L are not included in this pool; they remain external comparators.
The NNI operator itself is initializer-agnostic, so the operator audit also
applies it separately to every external constructor to measure the local and
compound improvement left in its returned tree.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
The primary metric is $\HT(G)$. Dendrogram purity is computed for the planted
fine and coarse partitions: for every same-class vertex pair, it measures the
class fraction under the pair's predicted LCA, then averages over pairs. We
also report the fraction of runs improved by one-step and compound NNI, number
of moves, runtime, and memory. For the exact suite we report the additive gap
$H^T_{\rm NEST}-H^*$, relative gap $(H^T_{\rm NEST}/H^*-1)100\%$, and
\emph{optimal-hit rate}: the fraction of graph instances whose objective is
within $\max(10^{-9},10^{-8}|H^*|)$ of the exact optimum. This objective metric
measures optimization accuracy independently of planted-tree recovery.

\subsection{Frozen benchmark results}
Table~\ref{tab:main-h} reports the tree returned by each standalone baseline
and the best verified endpoint from \NEST's declared three-initializer pool.
In particular, the \NEST row does not post-process HCSE or BBM outputs; their
NNI-refined variants are reserved for the operator audit below. \NEST has the
lowest mean $\HT$ in all five regimes and on \OursWins{} individual paired
graphs. Its paired reduction is
$\NESTGainVsHCSE\!\pm\!\NESTGainVsHCSECI$ bits relative to HCSE and
$\NESTGainVsBBM\!\pm\!\NESTGainVsBBMCI$ bits relative to BBM; it is lower on
all 500 graphs against each method. Figure~\ref{fig:entropy} shows paired
evidence against the stronger of HCSE and BBM on each graph. For graph $g$, we
define the winning margin
$M_g=\min(H^T_{\mathrm{HCSE},g},H^T_{\mathrm{BBM},g})-H^T_{\mathrm{NEST},g}$;
hence $M_g>0$ means that \NEST has lower entropy than both baselines.

\subsection{The operator audit}
The audit has two stages. One-NNI descent repeatedly commits the best
entropy-decreasing move until none remains. The two-move escape then tests
bounded pairs that may cross a small intermediate barrier, accepting only a
lower-entropy endpoint. Table~\ref{tab:operator} and Fig.~\ref{fig:runtime}
report the entropy removed, fraction of graphs improved, and refinement time.

One-step NNI improves every SE-agglomerative and oracle-BBM tree. After the
one-step certificate, the two-move escape further improves 62\% and 93\% of
those trees, respectively. For Paris, the one-step and two-move rates are 74\%
and 41\%; for HCSE they are 3\% and 4\%. The result shows that constructor
quality and NNI-local optimality measure different properties of a hierarchy.

\subsection{Components and exact optimality}
\NEST forms a small candidate set from SE agglomeration, recursive SE, and
Paris, refines each with the same exact operator, and returns the lowest
independently rescored endpoint. The main benchmark contains 3,500 method
records and 500 generator manifests. Its verifier checks hashes, schema, paired
seeds, unique keys, and monotonicity of committed NNI endpoints.

On a separate frozen 50-graph subset, candidate pooling improves 34 graphs,
one-NNI descent improves 45, and the two-move escape adds gains on 30.
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows that each component contributes to the final
result.

For the global audit, disjoint calibration fixes $q=32$, after which we seal
250 graphs at $n=12$, 100 at $n=14$, and 25 at $n=16$ across the same five
regimes. Every method receives exactly 32 candidates: coalescent starts for
\NEST, calls over target heights 2--5 for HCSE, and calls over a predeclared
cluster-count grid for label-free BBM. The deterministic \NEST pool is excluded
from this matched budget. Selection uses only structural entropy; the exact
dynamic program is consulted afterward.
Table~\ref{tab:exact-scale} shows that \NEST reaches \ScaleCombinedExact{}
optima, versus 2/375 for HCSE and 1/375 for label-free BBM. The table reports
exact binomial intervals at every graph size. All completed blocks have status
zero, matching SHA-256 seals, complete seed coverage, finite outcomes, and
independently recomputed summaries. The combined result is 370 exact optima
across 375 independently generated audit graphs.
